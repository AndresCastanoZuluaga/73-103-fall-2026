Course description

Macroeconomics seeks to understand why some countries are rich and others poor; why some countries have high rates of inflation while others maintain stable prices; why countries experience phases of (sometimes rapid) expansion and subsequent (sometimes sharp) contraction; and how fiscal and monetary policy can reduce the frequency and severity of these episodes.

In this introductory class to macroeconomics, we will only begin to address these questions so that you, as a business major, have the ground for understanding how macroeconomics can help you to make business decisions. The course will also introduce more advanced and recent topics that can help you understand the determinants of long-term economic growth, such as AI and its potential effect on jobs and productivity, and the implications of digital currencies (stablecoins) for monetary policy and financial markets, while also discussing recent developments with significant potential to influence short-term economic growth, such as tariffs.

While the topics covered in this course may appear distinct from those in an introductory economics class, macroeconomics fundamentally builds upon microeconomic principles. Throughout the course, you will frequently encounter concepts like demand and supply diagrams, elasticities, and microeconomic reasoning and intuition, especially as we progress beyond the measurement section of the course. These tools will serve as a foundation for understanding macroeconomic phenomena in greater depth once we start the chapters on short-term macroeconomic fluctuations and their causes.

Learning objectives

At the end of the course, students should be able to:

  • Understand the basics of macroeconomic measurement.
  • Calculate macroeconomic aggregates like GDP, CPI, and GDP deflator, interpret movements in these variables and their implications for businesses, as well as their limitations.
  • Analyze and explain patterns of unemployment and employment over time and across countries.
  • Elaborate on the economic function of banks and the government in regulating banks.
  • Describe the history, mission, and functions of the Federal Reserve Bank (Fed) and similar monetary authorities worldwide.
  • Understand the essential sources of inflation and the policy options available to monetary authorities to contain inflation.
  • Explain the causes of economic fluctuations and the role of policy in stabilizing these fluctuations.
  • Understand how equilibrium in goods and financial markets is achieved, the relevance of fiscal and monetary policy in the process, and the implications for governments, consumers, and businesses.
  • Use production functions to make sense of patterns of economic development and growth.
  • Understand recent developments that may shape the future of macroeconomics, such as AI and its impact on jobs and productivity, and digital currencies and stablecoins and their implications for financial markets and monetary policy.

Guidelines for Late or Missed Work

We do not accept late submissions for quizzes or homework. There are two reasons for this. First, we will post or discuss solutions within 3-5 days after the deadline. Grades will be available within 5-10 days after submission. Second, to be fair to everyone, we want to give all students the same amount of time to complete the work (and it is impossible for us to verify whether work was done late or just turned in late).

There is no make-up opportunity for missed quizzes. If issues prevent you from submitting the quiz during the designated time window, the lowest quiz grade will be dropped.

There is no make-up opportunity for missed midterms. Therefore, if you miss the midterm for any reason (excused or unexcused), its weight will be shifted to the final exam. If you miss the midterm, the final exam will be worth 55% of the overall course grade. The final exam will be on the day designated by the university. There will be a makeup for the final exam on the designated makeup day provided by the university.

QBA Classroom Conduct — Fall 2026

The Qatar Business Administration Program maintains high expectations for student classroom conduct. Your professors will hold you to these standards. We believe these standards create an environment most conducive to learning. This behavior also reflects common business culture. As CMU Qatar Business Administration students, part of your business education is to model the behavior that you will demonstrate in the workplace.

QBA students attend all scheduled classes. Unless the professor explicitly states that other ways of completing the work are acceptable, you must attend all scheduled classes. If you are unable to attend class, you are expected to contact the faculty member in advance. If you miss class, contact your professor as soon as possible. It is your responsibility—not the professor’s—to ensure that any assignments, handouts, notes, etc., are obtained before the next scheduled class.

QBA students attend class on time. In common business culture, punctuality is essential to showing respect for your colleagues and business partners. Showing up late for a meeting tells others involved that you do not value their time. QBA students will demonstrate respect for their courses, classmates, and professors by arriving for class early enough to get settled and prepared before the scheduled meeting time. The consequences of tardiness, absence from class, or late work are at the professor’s discretion and may affect grades. Specific policies can most often be found in your syllabus. To avoid disruption, professors reserve the right to deny late students’ admission to the classroom. Faculty members are expected to be alert to absenteeism that is detrimental to a student’s performance and to bring such cases to the attention of program administration.

QBA students remain in class once it has begun. Just as arriving late for a class, meeting, or presentation disrupts, so, too, does leaving in the middle. Professors, managers, and speakers may take it as a sign of disrespect or disinterest. You know your class schedules well in advance, so be sure to set your priorities in a way that reflects your responsibility to your educational goals. The class day provides many opportunities for breaks; you should rarely require one in the middle of a class session. In some specific instances, by your professor’s policy, you may not be allowed to reenter the room if you leave for exams and quizzes, for example.

QBA students come to class prepared. Your professors will expect you to arrive in class prepared for that day’s work. You will be expected to have completed the readings, case preparations, and other assignments prior to each class session. A Tepper business education is not a passive process; to get the most from your education, you must actively engage the process at every turn. You will have many opportunities to participate in your classes in a variety of ways, and if you have not done the work, you will have little to offer. In some cases, final grades have a class participation component. You can reasonably expect “cold-calling” in some of your classes. Preparation also includes arriving with the materials that you will need. Bringing a notebook, writing implement, texts, and other materials signals to your professor and classmates that you are ready to engage that day’s topic; arriving empty-handed signals the opposite.

QBA students use electronics in the classroom only for class purposes. Laptops are to be closed. When class is in session, you may use your laptop only as directed by your professor. You should not check email, tweet, text, play games, or surf the Internet, any activity that diminishes your or your classmates’ engagement with the classroom content and process. If you are unsure whether a given activity is appropriate, ask your professor. This policy extends to all electronic devices. Be sure that your phones and tablets are silenced and stowed before the class begins. Professors may add specific limits on sharing or using personal electronics during exams.

QBA students adhere to the CMU Policy on Academic Integrity. As a Carnegie Mellon University, Qatar Business Administration student, you will be held to the highest standards of academic integrity. Your professors and others will help you to fully understand what this means, but in the simplest terms, representing someone else’s work as your own or helping someone else to do so violates academic integrity. For example, not crediting a source in a research paper, copying a classmate’s homework, and sharing answers on a quiz are plagiarism or cheating, violations of the rules of academic integrity. Providing or using unauthorized graded work and answer keys from previous course offerings constitutes “Unauthorized Assistance;” students providing the materials and students receiving the materials both are violating academic integrity standards. Taking credit for the work of teammates while contributing none, sometimes called “freeriding,” likely violates academic integrity standards in that course. Be aware that the university policy on academic integrity, in addition to providing institution-wide standards, gives instructors the ability to determine what is appropriate in each of their courses. It is absolutely crucial for students to know the expectations for each course and to ask before they act when expectations are unclear.

Please see CMU’s general policies on cheating and plagiarism: https://www.cmu.edu/policies/student-and-student-life/academic-integrity.html

Your business faculty is dedicated to creating avenues for your academic success; we want you to get the most out of your educational experience at CMU. However, should you choose to disregard university integrity standards, the faculty is committed to closely following university and campus policies for reporting violations and pursuing appropriate academic disciplinary actions. The university will tolerate no violation. Penalties can severely impact your success in a course or even progress toward your degree. Before you make a bad decision, speak to a professor and find a better path. All of your academic and behavioral responsibilities are detailed in the Q-Word Student Handbook and the CMU Undergraduate Catalog, both available online. You can find answers to many of your questions there.

Use of Generative AI Tools

Certain assignments in this course will permit or even encourage the use of generative artificial intelligence (AI) tools, such as ChatGPT, Grok, and others. When AI use is permissible, it will be clearly stated in the assignment prompt posted in Canvas. Otherwise, generative AI is disallowed by default. In assignments where generative AI tools are allowed, you must appropriately acknowledge and cite their use. For instance, if you generated the whole document through ChatGPT and edited it for accuracy, your submitted work would need to include a note such as “I generated this work through ChatGPT and edited the content for accuracy.” Paraphrasing or quoting smaller samples of AI-generated content must be appropriately acknowledged and cited, following the guidelines established by the APA Style Guide. It is each student’s responsibility to assess the validity and applicability of any AI output that is submitted. You may not earn full credit if inaccurate or invalid information is found in your work, and you may be called into the professor’s office to explain and defend your work if unauthorized use of AI is suspected, which could lead to getting no credit for your assignment and being reported for academic integrity. Deviations from the guidelines above will be considered violations of CMU’s academic integrity policy. Note that expectations for “plagiarism, cheating, and acceptable assistance” on student work may vary across your courses and instructors. Please email me if you have questions regarding what is permissible and not for a particular course or assignment. Violations of this policy will be considered academic misconduct.

Two ways to think about the use of AI in this course. Depending on the assignment, AI use is accepted with caution (AI with caution) or not allowed. See below:

YELLOW LEVEL: AI with caution. AI tools may be used only for specific, limited purposes. The final work must be substantially students’ own creation. Appropriate for assignments like problem sets (AI for concept clarification, not solutions); case study analysis (AI for background information only); literature reviews (AI for source organization, not interpretation). In this course, that is the problem sets and debate background, when the assignment says so.

Do Don’t
Ensure you have your instructor’s approval to use AI Hide that you used AI
Use AI only for allowed purposes Copy AI responses without understanding
Show your own critical thinking Let AI write your analysis
Document how you used AI Skip documenting AI use
Verify all AI-provided information Forget to fact-check AI information
Cite any information sources you used Use AI beyond specified limits
Make the work substantially your own Rely on AI for the main argument

RED LEVEL: No AI assistance is permitted for this assignment. Applies to in-class exams and quizzes. No AI assistance is permitted for this assignment; it’s all about students’ individual skills and knowledge. These assignments help you develop confidence in your own abilities and allow the professor to assess your real understanding. All work must be completed independently using only approved course materials and resources as outlined.

Do Don’t
Use only your own knowledge Use any AI tools or chatbots
Rely on approved course materials Ask AI for help or ideas
Ask instructor for clarification Risk academic integrity violations
